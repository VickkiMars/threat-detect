#### 4.9.3 — Discussion Paragraph After Table 12 — REPLACE

The discussion paragraph after Table 12 currently states that the Hybrid CNN-LSTM
achieved ~86.80% accuracy on CICIDS2017 and the LSTM-only ~89.93%.
These figures are incorrect. Replace the entire discussion paragraph with:

---

Table 12 reveals two distinct performance regimes. On the primary UNSW-NB15
benchmark, all architectures achieved strong intrusion detection performance, with
deep learning models (CNN-only: 93.70%, LSTM-only: 93.62%, Hybrid CNN-LSTM: 92.64%)
substantially outperforming classical baselines (Decision Tree: 87.67%, SVM: 88.16%).
The quantized TFLite Hybrid retained 92.46% accuracy while reducing inference latency
to just 0.007 ms per 10-flow window — more than 6,900 times faster than the Random
Forest baseline (48.36 ms), which nearly saturated the 50 ms NFR1 ceiling.

The CICIDS2017 domain-transfer evaluation reveals a fundamentally different picture.
All six models converged to near-chance accuracy (48.1%–49.8%) when evaluated
zero-shot on the CICIDS2017 feature space. This outcome is not a failure of the
models, but an expected and scientifically valid consequence of feature-space
disjointness: the UNSW-NB15 training regime encodes flow representations across 38
UNSW-specific statistical attributes, while CICIDS2017 captures traffic through 78
distinct CICFlowMeter metrics. The learned latent PCA projections are not mutually
transferable without a shared feature extraction schema or domain adaptation. False
Positive Rates on CICIDS2017 ranged from 51.9% (Decision Tree) to 95.3% (SVM),
confirming that the decision boundaries established during UNSW-NB15 training carry
no discriminative signal in the CICIDS2017 representation space. This finding is
consistent with cross-dataset domain-shift literature and is candidly reported as a
limitation of this study in Chapter Five.

### Section 5.3 — Limitations

Find the Limitations sub-section. Add the following as a new numbered limitation
(or replace any existing text claiming CICIDS2017 was successfully generalised to):

---

Limitation — Cross-Dataset Feature Incompatibility:
The trained models are not directly portable to datasets with different feature
extraction schemas without domain adaptation. CICIDS2017 uses 78 CICFlowMeter
attributes that are structurally disjoint from UNSW-NB15's 38 flow statistics.
Zero-shot evaluation on CICIDS2017 yielded near-chance performance across all six
architectures, demonstrating that the learned PCA-reduced latent representations do
not transfer across feature-space boundaries. Future work should address this through
a unified feature extraction layer compatible with both CICFlowMeter and UNSW-NB15
schemas, or through few-shot domain adaptation on a small labelled CICIDS2017 subset.

---

### Section 5.4 — Recommendations for Future Work

Add or expand the following recommendations:

---

1. Unified Feature Extraction: Implement a shared network flow feature layer
   compatible with both CICFlowMeter (CICIDS2017) and UNSW-NB15 statistics to
   enable genuine multi-dataset training and cross-sensor portability.

2. Few-Shot Domain Adaptation: Fine-tune the Hybrid CNN-LSTM on a small labelled
   CICIDS2017 subset (e.g., 5,000-10,000 samples) to quantify how quickly the
   model adapts to a new feature distribution.

3. Multi-Class Attack Classification: Extend the binary threat classifier
   (Benign vs. Attack) to a multi-class model identifying specific attack
   categories (DDoS, PortScan, Brute Force, Web Attack, Infiltration).

4. Physical Raspberry Pi Deployment: Deploy the quantized TFLite model to a
   physical Raspberry Pi 5 cluster and measure actual power draw (mW), thermal
   throttling behaviour, and real ARM Cortex-A76 inference latency.
